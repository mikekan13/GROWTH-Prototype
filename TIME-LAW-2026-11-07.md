# THE TIME LAW — every act takes time (draft for Michael to mark up, 2026-11-07)

> Michael, 2026-11-07: "actions have to actually map to the time they take, and since time is fluid — days from one line or one second —
> all of the entities have to force whatever actions they're currently doing within that time." · "We are supposed to have time matter;
> that's why in combat you have a certain amount of actions in a set span of time, and it increases as you go up." · "It's not just
> reading speed, it's every possible thing you could do, in existence and out of existence." · "Skills are meant to beat things."

This is ONE law for every act a being can take — walking, reading, casting, forging, persuading, healing, flying, unmaking a star.
Reading is only the first case worked through. Nothing below is specific to any one act.

---

## 1. The clock is one clock

- A **round** is 6 seconds. That is the unit everywhere, not only in combat.
- The Watcher's line sets the **span** of a beat: one second, a round, an afternoon, three days. The span is whatever the flow of the
  story says (stated, or the engine's estimate — `lib/stated-duration.ts`).
- A beat of S seconds holds **S ÷ 6 rounds**.
- Combat is not a separate system: it is this clock with every beat fixed at one round.

## 2. Pace: what a being gets done in a round (attributes only — no roll)

For any act:

```
done per round  =  actions per round  ×  yield per action

actions per round  =  Σ over pillars of max(1, floor(pillar attribute levels / 75))        ← canon (2026-10-29)
yield per action   =  ordinary yield of the act  ×  (governing attribute / 30)  ×  trait/spell/condition factors
```

- **Actions per round** — already canon. An average human (≈30 per attribute) has 3: Body, Spirit, Soul.
- **Ordinary yield** — what an average human (governing attribute 30, nothing special) gets done with ONE action of this act.
  Movement is canon: Celerity 30 → 6 squares (30 ft). Reading: ≈ 8 words. Anything without a stated yield is estimated by the
  adjudicator *in ordinary-human terms* ("an ordinary person needs about 40 seconds to tie this knot"); a spell, item or skill can
  carry its own.
- **Governing attribute** — the attribute that speeds this act. When the act belongs to a skill, the skill's GOVERNORS name it
  (the same attributes Effort is drawn from). Unskilled acts: the adjudicator names the attribute that fits. Linear, no ceiling,
  exactly like Celerity ÷ 5 — a Wit 1000 being reads ~33× an ordinary yield per action and has many more Soul actions besides.
- **Traits, spells, conditions** — multiply the yield directly, no roll: a Nectar of quick hands, a spell of quickened sight, a
  Thorn that slows the mind, darkness, drink, exhaustion, an unfamiliar language.

**Pace is routine.** Doing an act at your own pace is ordinary competence: no roll (ruling 2026-10-30, rolls only at real resistance).

## 3. Skills: beating time (a check only when you push past your pace)

Skills keep their one job — beating a DR. They do not have a speed curve.

- Trying to do an act **faster than your pace** is resistance; time is the thing resisting. It is a check.
- **DR scales with the speed-up** asked for (2× modest … 10× near-impossible for an ordinary being). The DR ladder is OWED.
- **Relevance** applies as it already does: Speed Reading is highly relevant (−3) to reading fast; Literacy slightly; unskilled =
  FD + Effort only. A specialised skill earns its name: useless for doing it *well*, excellent for doing it *fast*.
- **Effort is spent either way**, from the skill's governors — pushing yourself tires you.
- **Success:** the act fits the shorter time. **Failure:** it takes your normal pace — or a cost (lost detail, a stumble). [QUESTION]
- 20 is godlike: a d20 skill die beats DRs nobody else can, so a master compresses time nobody else could.

## 4. Fitting the beat: budget, carry-over, interruption

- Each beat, every being in play has a **budget** = rounds in the span × its actions per round.
- Whatever it is doing is paid from that budget. What fits **completes**; what does not becomes an **ongoing act** on its record:
  what it is in the middle of, and how far along (read 2 of 5 messages; 3 of 8 metres crossed; 40% of the sigil drawn).
- Next beat, the ongoing act **resumes** from where it stopped — unless something interrupts it (a knock, a call, a blow, a choice
  to do something else). Partial progress is real data and stays true.
- A long span (a time jump) lets ongoing acts finish; every crystallized character lives the stretch (rulings 10-24, 10-31).
- A being doing several things in one beat spends the budget across them in order (pillars as canon allows).

## 5. Worked examples (only to show the law is one law)

| Act | Ordinary yield / action | Governor | Violet (Wit 32, Cel 22, 3 actions) | Wit/Cel 1000 being |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read | ≈ 8 words | Wit | ≈ 8½ words/action ≈ 25 words/round | ≈ 267 words/action, many actions |
| Walk/run | 6 squares (30 ft) | Celerity | ≈ 4 squares/action | ≈ 200 squares/action |
| Anything else | adjudicator's ordinary-human estimate, or the spell's/item's own | the skill's governors | scaled the same way | scaled the same way |

In a 5-second beat ("he stands and crosses the room"), Violet gets ≈ 2½ actions: she reads about 20 words of a 60-word text, and the
rest is an ongoing read next beat.

## 6. What the engine records

- **The act's cost** in actions, the **budget** spent, and **progress** on anything unfinished — rows, not prose.
- Message records (`WorldMessage`, built 2026-11-07) stamp each message's own read time, so "read 2 of 5" is two stamps.
