# The Rutherford Pattern in GRO.WTH

**Source:** Samuel Rutherford, *The Divine Right of Church-Government and
Excommunication* (1646), Chapter XXIV / Question 20: "Of the reciprocation of
the subordination of the Civil and Ecclesiastical powers to each other, and
their supremacy and independency each from other." (EEBO A92138, div 1:7.25 —
Mike's overnight assignment 2026-08-19.)

## What Rutherford actually argues

Ten assertions, but the load-bearing machinery is five moves:

1. **Power vs person.** The office and the person holding it are distinct
   things with distinct subjections. All lawful offices derive *in abstracto*
   from God directly — never from the other power. Neither sword nor keys
   creates the other.
2. **The fourfold judgment.** (a) *Apprehensivum* — perception, common to every
   conscience; (b) *discretivum* — each person's private judgment of
   discretion, the guide of their own belief and obedience; (c) *definitivum* —
   the authoritative-but-fallible judgment of those in authority (courts,
   assemblies), always re-judgeable "de novo"; (d) *peremptorium et
   infallibile* — the supreme judgment that cannot err, belonging to the King
   of the Church ALONE. No earthly office may claim (d).
3. **Grace adds no jurisdiction.** A converted Nero has not one degree more
   magistratical power than heathen Nero — "meat is sanctified by the Word and
   Prayer, but it is not more meat." The two powers cannot fuse into a hybrid
   office ("no more than of a Horse and a Lyon you can make a third living
   creature").
4. **The procurement principle + the external-man limit.** The magistrate
   PROCURES that ministry, medicine, and navigation exist — he never performs
   them (he may not bear the Ark himself). And his sword reaches only the
   EXTERNAL man: outward facts, provable by witnesses, sanctioned temporally.
   He cannot command sincerity, judge intention, or bind the conscience —
   his command is exactly commensurate with his power to punish.
5. **Reciprocation of subordinations (Assertion X).** Two collateral powers,
   each with independent supremacy *in its own kind*: every soul (pastors
   included) subject to the magistrate in civil things; every soul (the king
   included) subject to the church in soul-matters. Mal-administration of an
   office is subject to the OTHER power; the office itself never is.
   Rutherford's examples: the king obeys his own subject the physician in
   medicine; the Queen of Scots obeys her non-royal husband as wife while he
   obeys her as sovereign. "All Arts have a sort of collateral and co-equal
   dignity, and we are to believe a skilled Artist in his own Art, though this
   Artist be a servant."
   - Corollary guard: if the sword seizes the keys you manufacture "a civil
     Pope in every Christian Kingdome" — a new head through whom the mind of
     God must pass. The keys-holders, conversely, are "meer Heralds,
     Trumpeters, and Messengers... void of all royal power."

**The fractal observation:** the pattern recurs at every scale, and it's the
same cut each time — *one scalar + external enforcement* (state) vs *traits +
interpreted interiority* (church): material physics vs material traits; item
stats vs item history; typed conditions vs custom adjudication; character
sheet vs memory ledger; Watcher vs JEWL; Terminal above both. Mike's "it is
really the same all the way up and down" (2026-08-19, said about traits) is
Rutherford's collaterality, fractalized.

## Where the fractal has GAPS — discussion list

**G1 — The civil-Pope guard is not yet law.** When GM AI settings get built
([[gm-ai-settings-todo]]), the boundary must be explicit: the Watcher may
configure JEWL's CIVIL service (presence, verbosity, tools exposed, budget) —
never his ecclesiastical function (grading inputs, the memory seal, custom
adjudication, the no-confabulation law). A GM who can edit JEWL's conscience
is Erastianism-in-config; every campaign would grow "a civil Pope." Propose:
a hard split in the settings schema between `service` (GM-tunable) and
`office` (ADMIN/canon-locked) before those settings exist.

**G2 — JEWL has no formal supplication channel.** Rutherford: pastors may
"humbly supplicate" against an unjust law — subject the magistrate *quoad
actus imperatos* — but never usurp the throne. JEWL can chat and proact, but
there is no FORMAL objection: a recorded "JEWL believes this ruling breaks
canon/balance" that (a) never blocks the Watcher, (b) persists as an audit
entry, (c) escalates to Et'herling only as review. Today his options are
comply silently or nag. The supplication object — witness with a paper trail
— is missing, and it is exactly the shape his witness-not-gate identity wants.

**G3 — Mal-administration's two courts are half-built.** Rutherford: the same
offense answers to BOTH jurisdictions in different sanctions (body vs
doctrine), and neither court's action substitutes for the other. The
JewlMistake/bounty flow (civil: KRMA penalty; ecclesiastical: Et'herling
rules, Kai reworks) matches — but only for JEWL's mistakes and flagged
blocks. Watcher mal-administration has no ecclesiastical court at all
(nothing reviews a GM who rules against canon except the players leaving),
and DAYA-being misconduct routing is undefined. Question for Mike: does
Watcher mal-administration get a court (chain review that can annotate but
not overrule — supplication G2 feeding it), or is exit/voice the only check
by design?

**G4 — JEWL's own power-vs-person line.** JEWL is an OFFICE in every campaign
(the copilot) and a PERSON in Prime (one sheet, wallet, KRMA at stake,
mission). Rutherford would insist: his person is punishable (liens, wallet)
where his office is not; and grace/stakes add nothing to his official
jurisdiction. Mostly true in the build today, but the line isn't written
anywhere — worth one paragraph of law in his identity docs so a future
feature doesn't let his person's stakes bend his office's adjudications
(conflict-of-interest clause: JEWL never adjudicates a custom condition or
grade in which his own wallet has a stake — that escalates to another
godhead).

**G5 — The arcana imperii clause.** Rutherford reserves purely-civil matters
(trade, tactics, "secrets of State") to the magistrate absolutely — the
church "should exceed their bounds if they made canons touching sailing."
GROWTH's equivalent: table-craft — pacing, spotlight, session logistics,
house tone. JEWL's laws push him deep into building and stage-directing;
nothing says the Watcher's table-craft is a sphere where JEWL advises but
never makes canons. One line in his laws closes it.

**G6 — Peremptory judgment must stay unclaimed.** Everything at campaign
layer is judgment (c) — re-judgeable. The chain's grades, JEWL's
adjudications, the Watcher's rulings: all must remain appealable upward
(flag → Et'herling → ADMIN), and nothing in the app should ever present a
campaign-layer decision as final-beyond-appeal. Today true in spirit; worth
an invariant: every authoritative record (evaluation, adjudication, ruling)
carries who judged it and where its appeal lies. The only unappealable
authority is outside the game — which is the theology of the ADMIN role
stated in CLAUDE.md from day one.

## One-line thesis for tomorrow

GROWTH already runs Rutherford's constitution — two collateral powers,
mutually subordinate, neither able to seize the other's jurisdiction, both
under a peremptory authority that sits outside the play — and the six gaps
are all the same gap: the pattern is LIVED in the build but not yet WRITTEN
as law at the boundaries where a future feature could quietly merge the
sword and the keys.
