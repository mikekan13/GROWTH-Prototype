## DEATH = GRADUATION (the central upward mechanic of the economy)

Character death is **not** an ending or a punishment. It is the **graduation-and-ownership** hinge the whole "everyone eventually becomes an owner" thesis turns on.

When a Trailblazer's character dies, the player receives a **severance from custodianship** — emancipation from the GM's stewardship into ownership. The split:
- **Body KRMA** → dissolves back to the GM (the loan repaid).
- **Lady Death takes a cut** → for performing the transformation (officiating the graduation). *Transfer, not destruction — see correction below.*
- **Remainder → the Spirit Package** → now the player's, fully. The player **claims its KRMA outright** (it is no longer the GM's).

The Spirit Package is **published, authored canon**, not a tombstone:
- Resurrectable in the player's own campaign.
- **Bleeds into other campaigns** — appearing as an NPC, a PC, or a heavy *influence* on others' characters — attributed to the originating player via the DAG, **earning forever**.
- Death = *publication.* The character graduates from "my piece in one campaign" to "a permanent thread in the universe that pays me."

**Economic payoff — the sunset subscription:** by claiming character KRMA across deaths, a player can accumulate enough to **fund their own GMing without paying the subscription.** You rent the chair *until you own it.* The subscription was always designed to **sunset** — over time earnings exceed what you pay in. Death is a primary engine of this ascent.

Death graduates on all three layers at once:
- **Narrative:** the character ascends into the universe as canon (death-as-graduation, literal).
- **Economic:** the player graduates from custodianship into ownership of that character's KRMA.
- **Structural:** accumulated across deaths, it graduates the *player* from subscriber to self-funding GM.

## SELF-BALANCING TOWARD NEWCOMERS (canon property)

The system flows **more** KRMA toward newcomers/deficits to catch them up (the governance leak-diagnostic applied to people). A GM joining in year four starts with a small pool, and the system actively works to close the gap rather than letting early adopters compound forever. This is the *mechanism* that produces the **meta-year-10 endgame: all 100B dispersed roughly-equally across tables.** Concentration graduating into distribution over the decade — the economic arc *is* the thesis arc.

**Implication — the price may dissolve:** as flow-back catches up to flow-in (sunset-subscription logic, aggregate), participation may trend toward **effectively free** — post-scarcity emerging *inside GROWTH's own economy.* The product eventually dissolves its own price.

## THE VAULT AS COMPANY TREASURY (new addition to the money model)

Count the **Terminal vault** as a payout-eligible holder. Its profit-share **cannot be spent by a person**, so it becomes the **company's money** — retained earnings / net worth / savings (separate from operational flows like marketing, which are also KRMA-determined).

The shape self-tapers, no hard-coded percentage:
- **Early:** the vault holds most of the 100B → most profit flows to the **company** (funds the fragile early years, runway when players are few and costs high).
- **Late:** KRMA has flowed out to tables → the vault's proportion shrinks → the **company automatically takes less** as the community matures.
- The company's financial arc **emerges** from the vault's shrinking share — same emptying-vault curve as the distribution endgame.

**Lady Death's cut is drawn from this real-money vault** — tying her to the founder/company side (not skimming from players) and **pre-funding the eventual founder-exit severance.**

⚖️ Retained-earnings-via-vault and founder severance are corporate-structure/securities territory — concept clean, legal instantiation is counsel work.

## THE FOUNDER EXIT — "KILL LADY DEATH" ENDGAME (resolves D4: LIVE CANON, mechanics in rework)

The in-game **defeat of Lady Death is the founder's exit and the platform's decentralization.**

- Whoever's character kills her **assumes the title of Death** and **inherits that stake** — elevated in one move to something like a **major shareholder.**

This **IS the metamirror and the graduation thesis resolved in reality:** the creator removes his hand so the thing runs itself — answering Val's experiment ("can it self-sustain without me?") with his own company. The ultimate refusal to hoard: giving away not just the KRMA but the **throne**, with the succession mechanism written into the game itself.
